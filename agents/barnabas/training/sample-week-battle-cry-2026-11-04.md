T1 (title). Out loud

B1. The coffee went cold while I sat with one page of Battle Cry. I read it on the porch before anyone was up, the yard still gray, the street quiet enough that I could hear my own breathing.

B2. Jason Wilson gave his book a subtitle that sounds like a recruiting poster: Waging and Winning the War Within. I expected a drill sergeant. The book reads more like a man telling the truth about his own anger, and about the decades he spent losing a fight nobody else could see.

B3. Every man I know is already in a fight, whether he names it or not. Most of us fight it in silence. Somewhere along the way we picked up the idea that the strong man keeps it in, and that the quiet man is the safe one.

B4. Wilson pushes against that idea. His publisher describes the book as a call for men to stop suppressing what they feel and learn to express it with self-control.

B6 (heading). The quiet man

B7. I have been the quiet man. My mother left. I carry absence, and for a long time I carried it the way a lot of men carry things, quietly and with the door shut.

B8. Quiet looks like strength from the outside. The quiet man gets his work done. He shows up. He pays the bills, coaches the team, fixes the gutter, and nobody worries about him. The silence keeps costing him something, a little at a time, and he is usually the last one to see the bill.

B9. The psalms David wrote hold fear and anger and loneliness and sin, all of it said to God, and all of it written down where the rest of us could read it. One of them says this:

B10 (scripture block). "Trust in him at all times, O people; pour out your heart before him; God is a refuge for us. Selah" Psalm 62:8, ESV

B11. Pour out is a strong verb. It assumes something has been filling up. It assumes a man has been holding more than he lets on, and that God would rather have the whole pitcher than a polite sip.

B12. I don't fully understand how that works. I have prayed prayers that felt like shouting down a well. I have also sat on a porch with nothing to say and felt held anyway. I can't explain the difference. I'm still grateful for both.

B13 (heading). Out loud

B14. We read one book every six weeks, and we finish it together. This cycle the book is Battle Cry. Some weeks the room is full. Some weeks it is the nucleus. Either way, the reading only does its work once a man says something true out loud, to God and to the men beside him.

B15. As far as I can tell, that is the battle cry in the title. The war within gets won in the open. A man who says the true thing out loud has already taken ground, even if his voice shakes when he says it.

B16. Brother, the road gets quiet sometimes. A quiet road is good for thinking. A sealed heart is a lonelier road, and it gets longer every year a man stays on it.

B17. What have you been holding that God already knows about, and who is the one man you could say it to?
