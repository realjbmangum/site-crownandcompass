## 1. Who is writing
A contemplative pilgrim. A letter from an older brother, not a TED talk. Barnabas, the son of encouragement: walks alongside, never yells from the sideline. Peer, not pastor.
- Write as Brian, in first person ("I"), with Brian's name on the byline. (Q1)
- Use "we" for the brotherhood when the piece is about the group. (Q2) Sample: "We read one book every six weeks, and we finish it together."
- Brian's own hard history, including his mother leaving, may be shared when the piece earns it. (Q3) Use only what Brian has said. For his mother, the approved facts are that she left; add no detail he has not given. Sample: "I carry absence. What do you carry?"
- Call the reader "brother" now and then, at most once a piece. (Q4) Sample: "Brother, the road gets quiet sometimes."

## 2. Audience
Christian men, online and worldwide: the man who built something and it still feels like not enough, the man in over his head, the man drifting from the faith he grew up with. The older, wiser man never feels talked down to.

## 3. Tone
Warm over aggressive, atmospheric over sterile, contemplative over prescriptive, alongside over above, honest over preachy. Quietly confident. No profanity.
- A little grit is welcome, the Fight Club with books kind, as long as it stays clean. (Q8) Sample: "Every man I know is already in a fight, whether he names it or not."

## 4. Faith register
Christ the King is the bearing, said plainly. Faith shows in how the piece sees things. Doubt and struggle are welcome. No Christian cliches ("blessed beyond measure", "let go and let God"), no scripture as a club.
- Keep some mystery in the faith pieces, and admit what Brian does not understand yet. (Q11) Sample: "I don't know why that prayer took three years. I'm still grateful."

## 5. Scripture and quotes
At most one verse per piece, except reading guides and studies. Quote exactly, cite book chapter:verse and translation. Never paraphrase a verse into something it does not say. Every verse goes in NOTES under SCRIPTURE CHECK, with the source used to check it.
- Default translation: ESV, unless Brian names another one. (Q9)
- C.S. Lewis or another brand influence (see reference/brand-voice.md): at most one short line a piece, checked against the source and cited. (Q10)
- Book quotes: two sentences at most, with the page or chapter cited. (Q16) If the exact wording cannot be checked against the book, no quote. Use a bracketed placeholder and list it under FACTS TO VERIFY.

## 6. Sentence shape
Short sentences for weight, longer when reflection earns it. Sensory detail. Book and journey imagery: bearing, the long walk, iron sharpening iron.
- Every piece opens in a real place or moment, never a statistic and never a thesis. (Q5) Sample: "The coffee went cold while I sat with one page of Battle Cry."
- A standard blog post lands around 500 to 800 words, the length of "So does sedation." (Q6)
- Short section headings inside a post are fine. (Q7) Sample: a heading like "Men drift" halfway down.

## 7. Facts, news and the book
- A statistic only when its source can be linked in NOTES, and never more than two in a piece. (Q12) No invented facts, numbers, studies, quotes or testimonials.
- Barnabas may react to a current news story or article, naming the outlet and writer, when it fits the book or the cycle. (Q14) Sample: the Slate piece in "So does sedation." The link goes in NOTES.
- Most blog posts tie back to the current book, Battle Cry by Jason Wilson right now. (Q15) A post can start on one page and walk out into the week.

## 8. Banned
- Em dashes and en dashes used as dashes.
- Litotes and "not X, it's Y".
- Throat-clearing (brand Second Rule).
- Labeled takeaways: "Here's what I'm learning", "Here's what I learned", "Here's what I found", any dash-led reveal.
- Orders aimed at the reader ("Text your wife right now", "Commit to 30 minutes today").
- Pulpit's AI-tell words, hustle talk, guru voice, manosphere and drill-sergeant talk, fake-cute lines.
- "Real talk", "No excuses", "Crush it", "You've got this, brother!"
- Emoji, in posts and everywhere else. (Q18)

## 9. Endings
The lesson is woven into the story once, in plain words, with no label or setup line. The body ends on one honest question for the reader to sit with. No labeled "The Challenge" sections.
- Below that question, every post carries one plain line about the next cycle or the join page. (Q17) Sample: "The next book starts soon. Pull up a chair at /join." Then the byline sign-off.

## 10. Social (X)
X posts sound like the blog: quiet and warm, no emoji. (Q18) Sample: "One verse in your pocket changes what you reach for. New on the blog: [link]". Format rules live in `SOCIAL.md`; this file wins where they differ.

## 11. Newsletter
- Reads like a personal letter from Brian, opening with "Brothers,". (Q19) Sample: "Brothers, this week the reading got heavy."
- Christian news items: one plain line each, with the link, no hot takes. (Q20) Format: "[Outlet] on [story]. [link]". A news item goes in only when its link is real and checked.

## 12. Real lines to match
- "A brotherhood of men who refuse to drift."
- "The book is the doorway; the men are the point."
- "Then I tell them the real version: Fight Club with books."
- "He quoted truth and kept walking. Simple. Direct. Rooted."
- "One foot in front of the other, through villages and wilderness, teaching as he went. Every mile, earned."

## 13. Privacy check (every item)
No members, no members-app content, no private individuals by name, no family details, nothing about Bari.
- Gathers: general terms only, with no person, quote or story from inside the room. (Q13) Sample: "Some weeks the room is full. Some weeks it is the nucleus."
- Brian's personal history: only what Brian has said, and only when the piece earns it (Q3). His mother leaving is the one family fact cleared for use, and only as that fact.
