Discussion questions

Men's small-group discussion questions.

The right question is the difference between a room full of men agreeing about nothing and a room where the truth finally gets said out loud. This is a reusable framework you can bring to any men's small group or Bible study, plus real example questions for each part of the night. Steal it, adapt it, use it tonight.

Most men's groups do not fail because the men do not care. They fail because the conversation never gets past the surface. Someone asks "so what did everyone think of the chapter?" and gets a few polite nods, a couple of safe observations, and forty minutes of drifting off topic. Nobody lies, but nobody says anything real either.

Good questions fix that. A strong question is specific, it is answerable, and it leaves a man nowhere to hide behind a right answer. It moves the room from information ("here is what the author said") to confession ("here is where I am not living it"). You do not need to be a trained teacher to lead this. You need a handful of questions prepared ahead of time and the nerve to answer them honestly yourself first. Whoever is guiding sets the depth of the room by going first.

Below is a framework built around five moves: an opening check-in, digging into the reading, the honest and accountability questions where the real work happens, application for the coming week, and a prayer and close. Read a section each week the way a Crown and Compass chapter does, and bring three or four of these to your gathering. You will not get through all of them. That is fine. Depth beats coverage every time.

01

Check-in & opening

Do not open with "how's everyone doing." That question gets a performance. Open with something specific enough that a real answer is easier than a fake one.

  • Where are you, really, walking into tonight — one word for how the week actually went?
  • What is the one thing that took up the most space in your head this week?
  • Where did you see God at work in the last seven days, even if it was small?
  • What is something you are genuinely grateful for right now that you have not said out loud to anyone?
  • On a scale of running-on-fumes to full tank, where is your spirit tonight, and why there?
02

Digging into the reading

These keep the conversation anchored to the book or the passage instead of drifting into opinion. Start with what the text actually says, then move toward what it is asking of the reader.

  • What line or idea stopped you and made you re-read it? Why that one?
  • What did the author say that you wanted to argue with — and is your resistance about the argument, or about you?
  • If you had to put the main point of this section in one sentence to a friend, what would you say?
  • Where does this passage confront something our culture takes for granted about being a man?
  • Was there anything here that felt too easy or too familiar — something you nodded at but have never actually done?
  • What question did this chapter leave open for you that you are still sitting with?
03

Honest & accountability questions

This is where a men's group earns its name. These questions only work if whoever asks them answers first and answers straight. No fixing, no advice, no piling on. Just hearing.

  • Where in your life are you saying one thing and doing another right now?
  • What is the thing you would least want the men in this room to know about your last month?
  • Who in your house — wife, kids, no one — is getting the worst version of you lately, and why them?
  • What are you numbing with, when the day gets hard: the phone, work, food, a drink, a screen?
  • Last time we met you said you would work on something. Did you? Straight answer.
  • Where do you need to repent tonight, not in general, but the specific thing you already know?
04

Application this week

A discussion that changes nothing by Wednesday was entertainment. Close the reading by turning conviction into one concrete, checkable step per man.

  • What is the one thing this reading is asking you to actually do this week? Make it specific enough to fail or finish.
  • Who is the one person you need to go to — to apologize, to forgive, to encourage — before we meet again?
  • What is a small habit you could start this week that your future self would thank you for?
  • Where do you need to say no this week so you can say yes to what matters?
  • If nothing changes in your life over the next six weeks, what is the cost — and is that a price you are willing to pay?
05

Prayer & close

End by making the asks specific and the room a place men can lean on each other between gatherings. Vague requests get vague support.

  • What do you actually need this week — prayer, a text mid-week, a man who will ask if you did the thing?
  • Who wants to be the one to follow up with you before we meet again, and what will he ask?
  • What is one truth from tonight you want to carry out the door and into the week?
  • What can we thank God for together right now, out of what got said in this room?
