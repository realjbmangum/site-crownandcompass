Charleston, South Carolina

Christian Men's Group in Charleston, SC

Charleston wears its faith on the skyline — the Holy City, church steeples on almost every block, a spire in every direction you look. But a man can walk under all of those steeples on a Sunday and still not have one person who knows the harder parts of his week. Full pews are not the same as a few men who have your back. Crown & Compass is built for that second thing.

What it is

A small brotherhood, one book at a time

A Crown & Compass Chapter is a small group of four to eight men who read one book every six weeks and meet to walk it out together. It is Christ-centered and honest. Not a lecture, not a stage, not another thing to perform at. The book gives the group something real to push against, so the conversation gets past the tourist season and the tide chart into the things men actually carry.

The rhythm is simple and repeats: pick up a book together, read a bit each week and check in along the way, then hold a Gather at week six to close it out before the next one begins. Any man can lead a cycle, and the role rotates, so over time every Brother has taken a turn guiding the room. You can read the whole cadence on the how it works page, and see what we are reading now on the reading list.

Charleston, honestly

How to start or join a Chapter in Charleston

Here is the straight version, because a faith brotherhood should not start with a stretch. Crown & Compass has one Chapter right now, and it meets online. There is no room off Broad Street or over in Mount Pleasant with your name on the door yet, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

What that means for you in Charleston is a real invitation, not a waitlist. Join the online Chapter today and you are reading and meeting with Brothers this cycle, from wherever you are — downtown, West Ashley, James Island, or across the bridge in Summerville. And if you want men across a table in your own part of the Lowcountry, this is the good part: you can be the one who starts it. This is a city that knows how to gather on a porch and how to hold a place together through hard seasons. Start a Chapter, invite three or four you already half-trust, and the first local Charleston gathering happens because you called it.
