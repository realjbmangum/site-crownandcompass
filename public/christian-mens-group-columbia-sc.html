Columbia, South Carolina

Christian Men's Group in Columbia, SC

Columbia is a city that cycles men through it. Fort Jackson graduates a new class and moves the old one on; the University fills every August and empties every May; the State House hires and reassigns. A lot of good men pass a season here without ever landing in a room where anyone knows them by name. Crown & Compass is built for exactly that gap.

What it is

A small brotherhood, one book at a time

A Crown & Compass Chapter is a small group of four to eight men who read one book every six weeks and meet to walk it out together. It is Christ-centered and honest. Not a lecture, not a stage, not another thing to perform at. The book gives the group something real to push against, so the conversation gets past the Gamecocks score and the heat index into the things men actually carry.

The rhythm is simple and repeats: pick up a book together, read a bit each week and check in along the way, then hold a Gather at week six to close it out before the next one begins. Any man can lead a cycle, and the role rotates, so over time every Brother has taken a turn guiding the room. You can read the whole cadence on the how it works page, and see what we are reading now on the reading list.

How to start or join a Chapter in Columbia

Here is the straight version, because a faith brotherhood should not start with a stretch. Crown & Compass has one Chapter right now, and it meets online. There is no table waiting for you in Shandon or Five Points yet, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

What that means for you in Columbia is a real invitation, not a waitlist. Join the online Chapter today and you are reading and meeting with Brothers this cycle, from wherever you are — Forest Acres, the Vista, Lexington, or a barracks off Semmes Road. And if you want men across an actual table in your own zip code, this is the good part: you can be the one who starts it. This is a town that trains people to lead and takes discipleship seriously enough to have built a whole Bible college on its north side. Start a Chapter, invite three or four men you already half-trust, and the first local Columbia gathering happens because you called it.
